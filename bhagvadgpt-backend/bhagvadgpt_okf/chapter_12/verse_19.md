# Chapter 12, Verse 19

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
तुल्यनिन्दास्तुतिर्मौनी सन्तुष्टो येनकेनचित्।

अनिकेतः स्थिरमतिर्भक्तिमान्मे प्रियो नरः।।12.19।।

**English Translation:**
One who regards praise and criticism equally, who is thoughtful and restrained in speech, content with whatever comes, unattached to fixed possessions or residence, steady in mind and devoted, such a person is dear to Me.

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna continues describing the psychological traits of a mature and stable individual. One of the first qualities mentioned is the ability to treat praise and criticism equally. Many people build their self-worth around external approval. When praised, they become inflated with pride, and when criticized, they feel wounded or defensive. The person Krishna describes does not allow their identity to depend on the shifting opinions of others.

The verse also mentions restraint in speech. Being "silent" here does not simply mean avoiding words, but speaking thoughtfully rather than reacting impulsively. A disciplined mind learns to observe before responding, which prevents unnecessary conflict and emotional escalation.

Contentment with whatever comes reflects freedom from constant dissatisfaction. This does not mean abandoning effort or ambition. Instead, it means the person does not depend on specific conditions in order to remain mentally stable.

Krishna also refers to being "without fixed residence," which points to psychological non-attachment. The individual does not define their identity entirely through possessions, status, or external structures.

For Arjuna, these qualities represent the inner stability Krishna has been guiding him toward since the beginning of the dialogue.

**Modern Applications:**
freedom from external validation, mindful communication, contentment with circumstances, non attachment to possessions, stable identity
