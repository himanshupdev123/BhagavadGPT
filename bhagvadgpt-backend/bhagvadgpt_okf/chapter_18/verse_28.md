# Chapter 18, Verse 28

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
अयुक्तः प्राकृतः स्तब्धः शठो नैष्कृतिकोऽलसः।
विषादी दीर्घसूत्री च कर्ता तामस उच्यते।।18.28।।

**English Translation:**
The worker who is undisciplined, vulgar, stubborn, deceitful, malicious, lazy, morose and procrastinating is said to be a worker in the mode of ignorance (Tamasic).

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna concludes his diagnostic of the 'Doer' by describing the **Tamasic Operator**. If the Sattvic worker is a 'Master' and the Rajasic worker is a 'Hustler,' the Tamasic worker is the **Self-Saboteur**. This is the portrait of someone whose psychological 'operating system' has crashed.

He identifies eight symptoms of this paralyzed state:

1. **Ayuktaḥ (Disconnected):** They lack focus and discipline. They have no 'alignment' between their intentions and their actions.
2. **Prākṛtaḥ (Low-Level/Vulgar):** They act purely on base instincts. They are unrefined, lacking the intellectual or emotional maturity to handle complex tasks.
3. **Stabdhaḥ (Stubborn/Rigid):** They are unteachable. Even when their current methods are failing, they refuse to adapt or listen to feedback. This is the 'arrogance of the uninformed.'
4. **Śaṭhaḥ (Deceitful):** Because they lack the skill or effort to succeed fairly, they resort to manipulation, 'faking it,' or hiding their mistakes.
5. **Naiṣkṛtikaḥ (Malicious/Cynical):** They are 'haters.' They derive satisfaction from insulting others' work or dragging teammates down to their level of stagnation.
6. **Alasaḥ (Lazy/Lethargic):** They have zero 'kinetic energy.' They wait for things to happen rather than making them happen.
7. **Viṣādī (Morose/Depressive):** They dwell in a state of chronic dissatisfaction. They are always 'the victim' of circumstances, the boss, or the 'system.'
8. **Dīrgha-sūtrī (The Procrastinator):** This literally means 'making a short thread long.' They take weeks to do what should take an hour. They are the masters of 'Analysis Paralysis.'



Psychologically, Tamas is a protective shell. The procrastination and stubbornness are defense mechanisms against the fear of failure. If I never start (Dīrgha-sūtrī) and I never listen to advice (Stabdhaḥ), I can never be proven 'wrong.' It is a low-energy, high-misery existence that eventually leads to total obsolescence in a fast-moving world.

We all have 'Tamasic moments'—days where we just want to scroll mindlessly, ignore emails, and blame the world for our lack of progress. The danger is when these moments become your permanent identity. Which of these eight 'saboteurs'—perhaps Procrastination or Cynicism—sneaks into your workflow most often? Would you like to explore a 'Tamas-Busting' technique to kickstart your momentum when you feel this lethargy taking over?
