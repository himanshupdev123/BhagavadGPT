# Chapter 18, Verse 77

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
तच्च संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य रूपमत्यद्भुतं हरेः।
विस्मयो मे महान् राजन् हृष्यामि च पुनः पुनः।।

**English Translation:**
O King, as I remember that most wonderful form of Lord Krishna, I am struck with even greater wonder, and I rejoice again and again.

**Meaning & Purport:**
Sanjaya’s reaction to the Universal Form (Vishwarupa) highlights a profound psychological phenomenon: the enduring power of awe. While Chapter 11 described the actual vision, this verse focuses on the long-term impact that a 'peak experience' has on the human psyche. Sanjaya is not just remembering a visual image; he is re-entering a state of consciousness that transcends his immediate surroundings. 

In modern psychology, the emotion of awe is defined by two factors: a sense of vastness and a need to update our mental maps. When we encounter something truly great—be it a scientific discovery, a masterpiece of art, or a spiritual revelation—our ego shrinks. This 'small self' perspective is incredibly healing. It reduces stress and increases our sense of connection to the world. Just as Arjuna was humbled by the scale of the universe, Sanjaya finds that repeatedly recalling this vastness protects him from the petty anxieties of the palace and the impending war.

This verse teaches us the value of 'positive rumination.' While we often spend our time dwelling on past mistakes or future fears, Sanjaya consciously chooses to dwell on a moment of supreme clarity and beauty. By doing so, he sustains a high-vibrational mental state long after the event has passed. For a modern person, this suggests that cultivating and intentionally recalling moments of wonder can serve as a psychological anchor, providing a sense of perspective and joy that remains unaffected by external chaos.
