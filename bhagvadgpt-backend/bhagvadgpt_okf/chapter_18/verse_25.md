# Chapter 18, Verse 25

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
अनुबन्धं क्षयं हिंसामनपेक्ष्य च पौरुषम्।
मोहादारभ्यते कर्म यत्तत्तामसमुच्यते।।18.25।।

**English Translation:**
That action which is undertaken out of delusion, completely disregarding future consequences, loss, injury to others, and one's own capability, is said to be in the mode of ignorance (Tamasic).

**Meaning & Purport:**
After outlining the frictionless 'Flow State' (Sattvic action) and the exhausting 'Toxic Hustle' (Rajasic action), Krishna now hits the absolute basement of human execution: **Tamasic Action**. This is the anatomy of **Self-Sabotage** and **Blind Reactivity**.

In 2026, we might call this the destructive side of the 'YOLO' mindset, or acting when your prefrontal cortex (executive function) is completely offline. Krishna lists five specific markers of this disastrous operating system:

1. **Mohāt (Driven by Delusion):** The action doesn't stem from a logical plan or even a selfish ambition; it comes from cognitive fog, emotional flooding, or intoxication. You are acting out of pure, unrefined impulse.
2. **Anubandham anapekṣya (Blind to Future Consequences):** This is sending the explosive 2 AM email, making the reckless financial bet, or burning a bridge because it feels good *right now*. There is zero calculation of the 'Karmic Hangover' or downstream effects.
3. **Kṣayam (Disregarding Loss):** You act without auditing the ROI (Return on Investment). You are willing to blow up your reputation, waste massive amounts of time, or destroy your physical health for a fleeting hit of dopamine or revenge.
4. **Hiṁsām (Ignoring Collateral Damage):** You don't care who gets hurt in the blast radius. You become indifferent to the pain your chaotic actions inflict on your team, your family, or your own future self.
5. **Pauruṣam (Ignoring Your Actual Capacity):** This is delusional overpromising. You commit to projects, timelines, or arguments that you do not have the bandwidth, skills, or emotional regulation to handle.



Psychologically, Tamasic action happens when the ego feels so threatened or so exhausted that it bypasses all logic and goes straight into destructive survival mode. It is the ultimate failure of systems thinking. You aren't playing chess; you are just flipping the board.

If Sattvic action builds the future, and Rajasic action forcefully extracts from the present, Tamasic action actively burns down the past, present, and future simultaneously.

Think about a recent moment where you felt the intense urge to 'flip the board'—to send an angry text, quit a hard project on a whim, or make a reckless decision. Did you catch yourself before the blast radius expanded?
