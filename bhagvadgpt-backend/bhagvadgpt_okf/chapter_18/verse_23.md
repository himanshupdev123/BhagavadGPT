# Chapter 18, Verse 23

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
नियतं सङ्गरहितमरागद्वेषतः कृतम्।
अफलप्रेप्सुना कर्म यत्तत्सात्त्विकमुच्यते।।18.23।।

**English Translation:**
That action which is regulated and performed without attachment, without love or hatred, and without desire for fruitive results is said to be in the mode of goodness (Sattvic).

**Meaning & Purport:**
Having diagnosed the three types of *Knowledge* (Perspective), Krishna now shifts to the second pillar of human performance: *Action* (Execution). He begins by outlining the ultimate blueprint for sustainable, high-fidelity output: **Sattvic Action**.

In 2026, we constantly search for the perfect productivity hack to achieve the 'Flow State.' Krishna provides the exact four-part psychological protocol to enter flow on command and completely inoculate yourself against burnout:

1. **Niyatam (Regulated/Prescribed):** It is disciplined and consistent. It isn't driven by random bursts of chaotic 3 AM motivation; it is the steady execution of your core responsibilities. You show up and do the work simply because it is the work.
2. **Saṅga-rahitam (Without Attachment):** This is **Ego-Decoupling**. You do not merge your self-worth with the task. You are the operator of the machine, not the machine itself.
3. **Arāga-dveṣataḥ (Without Love or Hate):** This is the psychological state of **Task Neutrality**. You don't experience a manic high because you love a task (Rāga), nor do you procrastinate out of dread because you hate it (Dveṣa). You treat both writing a visionary strategy and doing your taxes with the exact same calm, objective focus.
4. **Aphala-prepsunā (Without Outcome-Thirst):** This is **Scoreboard Detachment**. You execute the play flawlessly, but you completely drop the desperate, thirsty obsession with the metric, the applause, or the algorithm.



Psychologically, this verse redefines burnout. We think we burn out because we work too many hours. Krishna argues we burn out because our work is infected with *Rāga* (chasing the high) and *Dveṣa* (running from the low). The emotional volatility is what fries your nervous system, not the physical typing on the keyboard. Sattvic action is deeply engaged, incredibly powerful, yet internally totally frictionless.

Think about the heaviest, most intimidating item on your to-do list right now. Is the friction coming from the actual physical effort, or from your emotional 'Rāga/Dveṣa' (attachment and aversion) toward it? Would you like to run that specific task through this 'Sattvic 4-Step Filter' to completely neutralize the dread before you start it?
