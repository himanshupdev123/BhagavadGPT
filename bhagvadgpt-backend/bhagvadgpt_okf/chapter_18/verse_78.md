# Chapter 18, Verse 78

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
यत्र योगेश्वरः कृष्णो यत्र पार्थो धनुर्धरः।
तत्र श्रीर्विजयो भूतिर्ध्रुवा नीतिर्मतिर्मम।।18.78।।

**English Translation:**
Wherever there is Krishna, the master of all mysticism, and wherever there is Arjuna, the supreme archer, there will also certainly be opulence, victory, extraordinary power, and morality. That is my opinion.

**Meaning & Purport:**
This final verse of the Bhagavad Gita presents a timeless formula for success, summarizing the entire dialogue. It describes a synergy between two essential forces: 'Yogeshwara' (the master of vision and wisdom) and 'Dhanurdhara' (the focused executor of action). Sanjaya concludes that victory is not a matter of chance; it is a psychological certainty when high-level strategy meets disciplined effort.

Just as Arjuna was a skilled warrior who became paralyzed without a clear 'why,' many modern individuals possess great talent but lack a guiding purpose. Conversely, having a great vision is useless if one is unwilling to do the heavy lifting. Success happens at the intersection of these two paths. Krishna represents the inner compass or the 'strategic mind,' while Arjuna represents the 'operational hand.' When your inner wisdom and your outer actions are perfectly aligned, you create a state of unstoppable momentum.

Psychologically, this verse highlights the importance of 'integrated leadership.' Whether in a corporation or in personal life, prosperity (Shri) and victory (Vijaya) follow when ethics (Niti) are combined with power. It moves us away from a 'hustle culture' mindset that values action for action’s sake. Instead, it teaches that the most effective way to live is to act with a sharp, bow-like focus while staying anchored in a deep, calm understanding of the bigger picture. This balance ensures that your success is not just profitable, but also sustainable and moral.
