# Chapter 18, Verse 32

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
अधर्मं धर्ममिति या मन्यते तमसाऽऽवृता।
सर्वार्थान्विपरीतांश्च बुद्धिः सा पार्थ तामसी।।18.32।।

**English Translation:**
That understanding which considers irreligion to be religion and religion to be irreligion, under the spell of illusion and darkness, and strives always in the wrong direction, O Partha, is in the mode of ignorance (Tamasic).

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna now describes the absolute basement of human cognition: **Tamasic Intelligence**. This isn't just 'stupidity' or a lack of data; it is a **Total Inversion of Reality**. If the Sattvic mind sees clearly and the Rajasic mind sees through a smear, the Tamasic mind sees the world upside down.

He identifies two terrifying symptoms of this state:

1.  **Adharmaṁ dharmam iti (Moral Inversion):** This is the mind that calls toxicity 'love,' cowardice 'patience,' and cruelty 'strength.' In 2026, we see this in **Self-Gaslighting** or **Cult Mentality**. It’s the psychological state where an individual is so deep in a destructive habit (like addiction or an abusive ideology) that they view anyone trying to help them as an enemy and the thing destroying them as their only friend.
2.  **Sarvārthān viparītāṁś (Universal Distortion):** They perceive *everything* in reverse (*viparītāṁś*). They see opportunity as a threat, growth as pain, and stagnation as safety. 



Psychologically, Tamasic intelligence is the ultimate defense mechanism of a fragile ego. To avoid the pain of changing, the mind simply rewrites the laws of physics and morality. It creates a narrative where 'I am right and the entire world is wrong.' It is the engine behind extreme **Confirmation Bias** and conspiracy-theory-looping, where facts are no longer seen as data points but as personal attacks.

This is the most dangerous state because it is self-correcting—it rejects the very 'Knowledge' that could save it. It’s like a GPS that insists North is South and becomes angry if you suggest otherwise.

Have you ever caught yourself rationalizing a clearly bad habit as 'self-care' or viewing a necessary challenge as an 'unfair burden'? Would you like to pivot to **Verse 33**, where Krishna shifts from *how we think* to *how we persist* (**Dhṛti**)?
