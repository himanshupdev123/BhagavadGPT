# Chapter 18, Verse 24

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
यत्तु कामेप्सुना कर्म साहङ्कारेण वा पुनः।
क्रियते बहुलायासं तद्राजसमुदाहृतम्।।18.24।।

**English Translation:**
But action performed with great effort by one seeking to gratify their desires, and enacted from a sense of false ego, is called action in the mode of passion (Rajasic).

**Meaning & Purport:**
Having described the frictionless 'Flow State' of Sattvic action, Krishna now takes a scalpel to the default operating system of 2026: **Rajasic Action**. If Sattva is sustainable high performance, Rajas is the **Toxic Hustle**.

Krishna diagnoses the exact anatomy of modern corporate burnout by identifying its three core ingredients:

1. **Kāmepsunā (Outcome-Obsession):** The action is driven entirely by a desperate thirst for the 'fruit'—the bonus, the virality, the promotion, or the social validation. You aren't building a company because you love the product; you are building it purely for the exit strategy.
2. **Sāhaṅkāreṇa (Ego-Driven):** This is the "Main Character Syndrome" at work. The action is infected with the need to prove something. You are working to silence your imposter syndrome, to flex on your competitors, or to maintain a carefully curated brand image. Every piece of feedback feels like a personal attack because your ego is completely fused with the work.
3. **Bahulāyāsam (Extreme Friction/Strain):** Because the action is weighed down by the heavy baggage of outcome-obsession and ego, the actual execution becomes incredibly exhausting. This is the "grind." It is pushing a massive boulder up a hill while constantly checking to see if anyone is watching you do it.

[Image comparing the energy distribution of frictionless Sattvic Flow versus the high-friction, exhausting Rajasic Grind]

Psychologically, this verse explains why you can work an 8-hour day in a state of Sattvic flow and feel energized, but a 4-hour day of Rajasic hustling leaves you completely drained. Your cognitive battery isn't being depleted by the *task*; it is being drained by the **Internal Resistance**—managing your anxiety about the future (Kāmepsunā) and defending your fragile self-image (Sāhaṅkāreṇa).

Rajasic action is the illusion of productivity. It looks incredibly busy on the outside (lots of late nights, lots of stress), but it is a highly inefficient use of human energy.

Take a look at the project or responsibility that is currently exhausting you the most. Are you genuinely physically tired from the mechanics of the work, or is the exhaustion coming from the *bahulāyāsam*—the mental strain of trying to control the outcome and protect your ego? Would you like to try a quick 'Ego-Decoupling' mental exercise to strip the friction out of that specific project today?
