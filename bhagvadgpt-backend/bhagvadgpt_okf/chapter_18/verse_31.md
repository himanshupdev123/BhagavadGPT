# Chapter 18, Verse 31

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
यया धर्ममधर्मं च कार्यं चाकार्यमेव च।
अयथावत्प्रजानाति बुद्धिः सा पार्थ राजसी।।18.31।।

**English Translation:**
O son of Pritha, that understanding which cannot distinguish between right and wrong, between action that should be done and action that should not be done, is in the mode of passion (Rajasic).

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna now identifies the operating system running most of the high-stress, high-stakes sectors of 2026: **Rajasic Intelligence**. If Sattvic intelligence is a high-resolution camera, Rajasic intelligence is a camera with a **Smeared Lens**. It knows there is a 'right' and a 'wrong,' but because it is clouded by personal desire (*Rāga*) and ego, it cannot see the boundary clearly.

He highlights the failure of discernment in two areas:

1.  **Dharma vs. Adharma (Moral Confusion):** This isn't about being 'evil'; it's about being **Convenient**. A Rajasic mind twists ethics to suit its current ambition. It uses 'situational morality'—convincing itself that a small lie or an unethical shortcut is actually 'fine' because it serves a 'good' goal (usually their own success).
2.  **Kārya vs. Akārya (Action Confusion):** This is the failure to prioritize. The Rajasic mind treats everything as an emergency. It cannot distinguish between a high-value strategic move and a low-value distraction that offers a quick hit of dopamine or status.



Psychologically, this is the realm of **Cognitive Bias**. The Rajasic mind sees things *ayathāvat* (not as they are). It sees reality through the filter of 'What’s in it for me?' This leads to 'The Hustler’s Delusion'—the belief that you can ignore the laws of health, ethics, or human connection to reach the top, only to realize you’ve built your success on a foundation of sand.

In modern life, this manifests as **Burnout-Inducing Overcommitment**. Because you can't clearly see what *not* to do (*akārya*), you say yes to everything. You confuse 'being busy' with 'being effective.'

Are you currently in a situation where the 'right' move feels blurry because of the potential reward at stake? Would you like to look at **Verse 32**, which describes the 'Tamasic' mind—the one that actually gets everything completely backward?
