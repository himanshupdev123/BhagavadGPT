# Chapter 18, Verse 21

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
पृथक्त्वेन तु यज्ज्ञानं नानाभावान्पृथग्विधान्।
वेत्ति सर्वेषु भूतेषु तज्ज्ञानं विद्धि राजसम्।।18.21।।

**English Translation:**
But that knowledge which sees in all living beings a variety of different, separate identities and entities, know that knowledge to be in the mode of passion (Rajasic).

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna now steps down the diagnostic spectrum to the operating system running most of the modern world: **Rajasic Knowledge**. If Sattvic knowledge (Verse 20) is the superpower of seeing the unified ecosystem, Rajasic knowledge is the lens of **Absolute Fragmentation**.

Krishna defines this mode precisely as *pṛthaktvena* (by separation) and *nānā-bhāvān* (multifarious, disconnected identities). In 2026, this is the default setting of our algorithms, our politics, and our corporate structures. 

When you view the world through a Rajasic lens, you suffer from two major psychological glitches:
1. **The Zero-Sum Illusion:** Because you see every individual as a completely separate entity competing for limited resources, life becomes a relentless, exhausting competition. If your colleague gets a promotion, you feel like *you* lost something. You cannot celebrate another's success because your brain registers it as a threat to your own survival.
2. **Hyper-Categorization (The "Us vs. Them" Trap):** You obsessively divide the world into categories: my team vs. their team, my ideology vs. their ideology, my generation vs. their generation. You lose the ability to see the shared human baseline (the *avibhaktam* from the previous verse).



Psychologically, living in Rajasic perception is a recipe for chronic, low-grade anxiety. Why? Because when you see everyone as fundamentally separate from you, you are constantly on defense. You must relentlessly manage your brand, guard your territory, and hustle to stay ahead of the pack. It is an incredibly lonely and high-friction way to operate.

This isn't just about being "nice"; it’s about **Cognitive Efficiency**. The Rajasic leader wastes massive amounts of bandwidth fighting internal office politics, whereas the Sattvic leader aligns the team toward a shared, unified goal.

Think about a relationship or an environment (like your workplace) where you feel constantly on edge, defensive, or fiercely competitive. Are you currently viewing that situation through the fragmented, zero-sum lens of Rajas? Would you like to run a quick cognitive reframe to shift back to a Sattvic, unified perspective and drop that exhausting friction?
