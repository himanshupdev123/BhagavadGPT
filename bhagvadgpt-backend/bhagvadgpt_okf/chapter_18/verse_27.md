# Chapter 18, Verse 27

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
रागी कर्मफलप्रेप्सुर्लुब्धो हिंसात्मकोऽशुचिः।
हर्षशोकान्वितः कर्ता राजसः परिकीर्तितः।।18.27।।

**English Translation:**
The worker who is attached to work and the fruits of work, desiring to enjoy those fruits, and who is greedy, always envious, impure, and moved by joy and sorrow, is said to be a worker in the mode of passion (Rajasic).

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna now provides the psychological profile of the **Rajasic Doer**. In 2026, this is the 'High-Stress Achiever'—the individual who looks successful on LinkedIn but is internally falling apart. This is the portrait of **Transactional Living**.

He identifies six symptoms of this exhausting operating system:

1. **Rāgī (Obsessively Attached):** They are fused with their work. Their job isn't what they do; it is who they are. If the work is criticized, they feel personally attacked.
2. **Karma-phala-prepsuḥ (Outcome-Thirsty):** They are addicted to the 'win.' They don't enjoy the process; they only tolerate it to get to the bonus, the title, or the applause. 
3. **Lubdhaḥ (Greedy/Hoarding):** Even when they win, it’s never enough. They suffer from 'Moving Goalpost Syndrome.' They are unwilling to share credit, resources, or knowledge because they view life as a zero-sum game.
4. **Hiṁsātmakaḥ (Envious/Exploitative):** To stay ahead, they are willing to step on others. This can manifest as subtle workplace sabotage, taking credit for a subordinate's work, or simply 'using' people as ladders for their own climb.
5. **Aśuciḥ (Impure/Unethical):** When the thirst for the result becomes too high, integrity becomes secondary. They start cutting corners, 'massaging' data, or compromising their values for the sake of the KPI.
6. **Harṣa-śokānvitaḥ (The Emotional Rollercoaster):** This is the most visible symptom. They are hyper-reactive. A good sales day makes them manic and arrogant; a single bad review sends them into a spiral of despair. They have no internal 'shock absorbers.'



Psychologically, the Rajasic doer is a slave to the external environment. Because they have no internal anchor, their happiness is outsourced to things they cannot control (market trends, boss's moods, social media metrics). This is why Rajasic success often leads directly to a mid-life crisis or severe burnout; the 'price' of the achievement was their peace of mind.

Do you recognize any of these 'Rajasic' traits in your current professional approach? Are you currently riding an 'Emotional Rollercoaster' where your mood for the day is entirely dictated by your results? Would you like to explore a specific 'Shock Absorber' exercise to help decouple your mood from your metrics today?
