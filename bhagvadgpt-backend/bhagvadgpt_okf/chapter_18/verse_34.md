# Chapter 18, Verse 34

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
यया तु धर्मकामार्थान् धृत्या धारयतेऽर्जुन।
प्रसङ्गेन फलाकाङ्क्षी धृतिः सा पार्थ राजसी।।18.34।।

**English Translation:**
But that determination by which one holds fast to duty, pleasure, and wealth, desiring the fruits of actions out of intense attachment, O Partha, is in the mode of passion (Rajasic).

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna now identifies the psychological fuel behind the 2026 'High-Achiever'—**Rajasic Determination**. If Sattvic grit is sustained by internal alignment, Rajasic grit is powered entirely by **Transactional Incentives**. 

He points out that the Rajasic person has plenty of willpower, but it is strictly reserved for the 'Big Three' of worldly success:
1.  **Dharma (Duty/Reputation):** Following the rules primarily to look good or maintain status.
2.  **Kāma (Pleasure/Gratification):** The drive to acquire comfort, experiences, and sensory hits.
3.  **Artha (Wealth/Power):** The relentless pursuit of resources and influence.



Psychologically, the keyword here is *phalākāṅkṣī* (desiring the fruits). This is **Outcome-Dependent Willpower**. The Rajasic individual can work 100 hours a week, but only if they believe a massive 'payoff' is coming. Their determination is a contract: "I will endure this pain *if* you give me that prize."

This creates a massive psychological vulnerability: **Fragile Persistence**. Because their grit is fueled by the external result, if the result is delayed, the market dips, or the 'likes' don't come in, their determination collapses into bitterness or burnout. They are incapable of 'showing up for the sake of showing up.' In 2026, we see this in 'grind culture'—it looks powerful, but it's internally brittle because it’s not fueled by the joy of the work, but by the thirst for the exit.

Are you currently working hard on a project because you love the process, or are you 'white-knuckling' it purely for a specific reward? Would you like to see **Verse 35**, which describes **Tamasic Determination**—the 'grit' that makes us stubbornly hold onto things that are actually destroying us?
