# Chapter 3, Verse 36

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
अथ केन प्रयुक्तोऽयं पापं चरति पूरुषः।

अनिच्छन्नपि वार्ष्णेय बलादिव नियोजितः।।3.36।।

**English Translation:**
Arjuna said: O descendant of Vrishni, by what is a person driven to commit sinful actions, even unwillingly, as if compelled by force?

**Meaning & Purport:**
This verse marks an important psychological question raised by Arjuna. After hearing Krishna’s teachings about self-control and righteous action, Arjuna observes a troubling reality: people often act against their own better judgment. Even when they know something is wrong, they still do it. He asks what force pushes a person toward such behavior.

Arjuna’s question reflects a universal human experience. Many individuals promise themselves they will avoid harmful habits, destructive choices, or unethical actions, yet they find themselves repeating the same patterns. It feels as if some inner force overrides reason and willpower. This confusion between knowledge and behavior is exactly what Arjuna is trying to understand.

At this moment in the dialogue, Arjuna is not speaking as a warrior but as a thoughtful observer of human nature. His question opens the door for Krishna to explain one of the Gita’s most important psychological teachings: the role of desire and anger as powerful forces that disturb the mind.

By raising this question, Arjuna represents the struggle of every person who tries to live wisely but feels pulled in the opposite direction by inner impulses. The verse therefore sets the stage for a deeper exploration of the forces that drive human behavior and the methods required to master them.

**Modern Applications:**
self-sabotage, loss of self-control, addiction and compulsive behavior, conflict between knowledge and action, inner psychological conflict, impulse vs reason
