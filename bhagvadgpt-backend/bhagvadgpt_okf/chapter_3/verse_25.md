# Chapter 3, Verse 25

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
सक्ताः कर्मण्यविद्वांसो यथा कुर्वन्ति भारत।
कुर्याद्विद्वांस्तथासक्तश्िचकीर्षुर्लोकसंग्रहम्।।3.25।।

**English Translation:**
As the ignorant perform their duties with attachment to results, O descendant of Bharata, the wise should act with the same zeal, but without attachment, for the sake of leading people on the right path.

**Meaning & Purport:**
This verse presents a profound psychological contrast between two mindsets: the attached and the detached. Krishna emphasizes that spiritual or psychological maturity does not mean doing less work. The wise and the ignorant may perform the exact same actions with the exact same intensity. The crucial difference lies entirely in their internal motivation.

Just as Arjuna felt paralyzed by his attachments and duties, mistakenly believing that escaping the battlefield would make him 'wise' or peaceful, modern individuals often fall into a similar trap. We assume that finding inner peace means withdrawing from ambition, slowing down to the point of stagnation, or abandoning our demanding responsibilities. We frequently confuse detachment with apathy.

Krishna corrects this misconception by stating that a wise person must work just as hard as someone driven by greed and ego. However, while the ignorant are fueled by a desperate, anxious need for personal validation, wealth, or status, the wise are fueled by a desire to contribute to the greater good and set a positive example. If the most capable and self-aware people simply quit working, society loses its best role models. Therefore, the ultimate goal is not to stop acting, but to shift the engine of your action from anxious, self-centered attachment to purposeful, detached service. By doing so, you maintain high performance without the psychological burnout.
