# Chapter 3, Verse 29

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
प्रकृतेर्गुणसम्मूढाः सज्जन्ते गुणकर्मसु।
तानकृत्स्नविदो मन्दान्कृत्स्नविन्न विचालयेत्।।3.29।।

**English Translation:**
Those confused by the modes of nature become attached to material activities and their results. The wise, who possess complete knowledge, should not unsettle these people of shallow understanding.

**Meaning & Purport:**
This verse addresses the psychological impact of knowledge and the ethical responsibility of those who possess it. It explains that people who are deeply conditioned by their environment and biological drives naturally become obsessed with material results. Their identity is tied to their productivity and possessions because they do not yet see the larger systems at play.

Just as Arjuna felt paralyzed by his attachments and duties, struggling to see beyond the immediate emotional trauma of the war, many people today are entirely driven by the 'rat race.' Their self-worth is tethered to external achievements. Krishna advises that a person with a deeper psychological understanding should not abruptly shatter the foundations of such individuals. 

If you tell someone who finds meaning only in their career that 'work is an illusion,' you may strip away their only source of stability and cause a psychological breakdown or a slide into laziness. Instead, the wise should lead by quiet example. The goal is to encourage others to evolve naturally rather than forcing a perspective they aren't ready to handle. This highlights a sophisticated form of empathy: recognizing that everyone is at a different stage of mental development and that pushing high-level truths onto an unprepared mind can be more harmful than helpful.

**Modern Applications:**
leadership, empathy, psychological safety, personal growth, mentorship, emotional maturity
