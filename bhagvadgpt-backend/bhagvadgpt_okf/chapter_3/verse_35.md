# Chapter 3, Verse 35

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
श्रेयान्स्वधर्मो विगुणः परधर्मात्स्वनुष्ठितात्।

स्वधर्मे निधनं श्रेयः परधर्मो भयावहः।।3.35।।

**English Translation:**
It is better to perform one’s own duty imperfectly than to perform another’s duty perfectly. Even death in the course of one’s own duty is better, for following another’s path is dangerous.

**Meaning & Purport:**
This verse presents one of the most important principles in the Bhagavad Gita: the importance of living according to one's own nature and responsibility, known as svadharma. Every person is born with certain tendencies, abilities, and roles shaped by their nature and circumstances. When a person follows their own path—even if imperfectly—it creates inner stability and authenticity.

Krishna explains this to Arjuna because Arjuna is tempted to abandon his duty as a warrior. Out of emotional distress, he wishes to avoid the battle and adopt a life that appears more peaceful. However, Krishna warns that abandoning one's true responsibility for a role that seems easier or morally safer can lead to deeper conflict and fear. Arjuna’s hesitation is understandable, but running from his duty would only create greater disorder within himself and in society.

This insight applies strongly to modern life. People often compare themselves to others and try to imitate careers, lifestyles, or identities that do not align with their own nature. Social pressure and comparison can push individuals away from their authentic strengths. Even if they succeed outwardly, the result may be anxiety, dissatisfaction, and a sense of living someone else's life.

The verse teaches that fulfillment comes from accepting one's role and working to perform it with sincerity. Authentic effort in one's own path is more meaningful than perfect performance in a path that does not truly belong to you.
