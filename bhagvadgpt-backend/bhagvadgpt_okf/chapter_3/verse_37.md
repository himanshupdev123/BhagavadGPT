# Chapter 3, Verse 37

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
श्री भगवानुवाच

काम एष क्रोध एष रजोगुणसमुद्भवः।

महाशनो महापाप्मा विद्ध्येनमिह वैरिणम्।।3.37।।

**English Translation:**
The Blessed Lord said: It is desire, it is anger, born of the quality of passion. It is all-devouring and greatly sinful. Know this to be the enemy here in this world.

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna directly answers Arjuna’s question about what force drives people to act against their own judgment. He identifies the root cause as desire (kama), which later transforms into anger (krodha) when that desire is blocked. These forces arise from the restless quality of human nature known as rajas, which constantly pushes the mind toward craving, ambition, and dissatisfaction.

Desire itself begins as a simple attraction toward something pleasurable—success, recognition, comfort, power, or emotional attachment. However, when the mind becomes dependent on fulfilling these desires, they grow stronger and more demanding. Krishna describes desire as "all-devouring" because it never reaches a point of satisfaction. Once one desire is fulfilled, another immediately replaces it.

When desires are frustrated, they turn into anger, frustration, and resentment. This shift disturbs judgment and can push a person into harmful decisions. Just as Arjuna wondered why people act against their own wisdom, Krishna explains that uncontrolled desire clouds clarity and weakens self-control.

This insight reveals a key psychological truth: the real enemy of clarity is not the external world but the uncontrolled impulses within the mind. Recognizing this inner force is the first step toward mastering it and regaining control over one’s actions.

**Modern Applications:**
uncontrolled desire, anger from frustration, impulse control, addiction to success or pleasure, emotional reactivity, inner psychological enemies, self-mastery
