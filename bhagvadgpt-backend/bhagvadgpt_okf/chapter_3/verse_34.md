# Chapter 3, Verse 34

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
इन्द्रियस्येन्द्रियस्यार्थे रागद्वेषौ व्यवस्थितौ।

तयोर्न वशमागच्छेत्तौ ह्यस्य परिपन्थिनौ।।3.34।।

**English Translation:**
Attachment and aversion exist in relation to the objects of each sense. One should not come under their control, for they are obstacles on the path.

**Meaning & Purport:**
This verse describes a basic mechanism of the human mind: attraction and aversion. Whenever the senses encounter objects, the mind naturally forms preferences. Some experiences create attachment (raga), while others create dislike or resistance (dvesha). These reactions are automatic psychological tendencies.

Krishna warns that the real problem begins when a person becomes controlled by these impulses. When attachment dominates, a person becomes dependent on certain experiences, people, or outcomes for happiness. When aversion dominates, the mind constantly avoids discomfort, criticism, or challenge. Both tendencies distort judgment and weaken self-control.

Arjuna’s crisis reflects this struggle. His attachment to family and teachers, combined with his aversion to the painful consequences of war, is clouding his ability to see his duty clearly. His emotions are understandable, but if he allows them to dictate his actions completely, they will block his ability to act according to dharma.

This teaching applies directly to modern life. People often make decisions based purely on what feels pleasant or unpleasant in the moment. They chase comfort and avoid difficulty, even when difficulty is necessary for growth. Krishna’s message is not to eliminate feelings, but to recognize them without becoming their servant. Clarity and disciplined awareness allow a person to act wisely instead of reacting impulsively.
