# Chapter 3, Verse 43

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
एवं बुद्धेः परं बुद्ध्वा संस्तभ्यात्मानमात्मना।

जहि शत्रुं महाबाहो कामरूपं दुरासदम्।।3.43।।

**English Translation:**
Thus knowing the Self to be superior to the intellect, steady the mind by the Self, O mighty-armed Arjuna, and conquer this difficult enemy in the form of desire.

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna concludes this section by giving Arjuna a clear strategy for overcoming the inner enemy of desire. After explaining the hierarchy of senses, mind, intellect, and the Self, Krishna now urges Arjuna to use this understanding in practice. The senses may be strong, and the mind may become restless, but the intellect has the power to guide them. Beyond even the intellect lies the deeper Self, the stable awareness that is not disturbed by passing impulses.

Krishna instructs Arjuna to stabilize himself by relying on this higher awareness. When a person remembers their deeper identity and purpose, impulses lose their ability to dominate behavior. Desire is described as a difficult enemy because it repeatedly returns and tries to regain control. Conquering it does not mean eliminating every desire but preventing it from ruling one's decisions.

Arjuna’s struggle on the battlefield is not only external but internal. His emotions and attachments were weakening his resolve. Krishna’s guidance shows that victory begins within. By strengthening clarity, awareness, and inner discipline, a person can regain authority over the impulses that disturb the mind.

This verse presents a final psychological principle: lasting self-control comes not merely from suppressing impulses but from aligning the mind with a deeper sense of identity, purpose, and awareness.

**Modern Applications:**
self-mastery, inner discipline, overcoming impulses, purpose-driven decision making, mindfulness and awareness, emotional self-control, mastering desires
