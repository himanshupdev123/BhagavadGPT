# Chapter 3, Verse 42

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
इन्द्रियाणि पराण्याहुरिन्द्रियेभ्यः परं मनः।

मनसस्तु परा बुद्धिर्यो बुद्धेः परतस्तु सः।।3.42।।

**English Translation:**
The senses are said to be powerful, but the mind is higher than the senses. Higher than the mind is the intellect, and higher than the intellect is the Self.

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna explains a hierarchy within human psychology that helps a person understand how self-control is possible. At the lowest level are the senses, which constantly seek pleasurable experiences from the external world. These sensory impulses are strong, but they are not the ultimate authority in human behavior.

Above the senses is the mind. The mind organizes sensory experiences, forms desires, and creates emotional reactions. However, the mind itself can be unstable and easily influenced by attraction or fear. Above the mind stands the intellect. The intellect has the ability to evaluate, reason, and decide what is right or wrong. It provides the capacity for reflection and deliberate choice.

Krishna then points to something even higher—the Self. This represents the deeper conscious identity of a person, the stable awareness that observes thoughts, emotions, and impulses. When a person becomes aware of this deeper level, they are no longer fully controlled by the restless movements of the senses and mind.

For Arjuna, this teaching offers a way to regain control over his confusion. His emotions and sensory attachments are disturbing his mind, but by using his intellect and reconnecting with his deeper awareness, he can regain clarity.

The verse presents a psychological strategy: by strengthening awareness and reason, a person can gradually guide and discipline the impulses that arise from the senses and the mind.
