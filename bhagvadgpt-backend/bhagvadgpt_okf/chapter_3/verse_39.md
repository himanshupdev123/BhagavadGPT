# Chapter 3, Verse 39

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
आवृतं ज्ञानमेतेन ज्ञानिनो नित्यवैरिणा।

कामरूपेण कौन्तेय दुष्पूरेणानलेन च।।3.39।।

**English Translation:**
O son of Kunti, knowledge is covered by this constant enemy of the wise, in the form of desire, which burns like an insatiable fire.

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna continues explaining the psychological power of desire. He describes it as a constant enemy because it repeatedly interferes with clarity and self-control. Even a person who understands what is right can struggle against this force. Desire does not disappear simply because someone has knowledge. Instead, it constantly tries to overpower judgment.

Krishna compares desire to fire that can never be fully satisfied. Just as feeding fire with fuel only makes it burn more strongly, fulfilling desires often leads to more desires rather than lasting satisfaction. The mind becomes trapped in a cycle of craving, temporary pleasure, and renewed craving. This endless hunger gradually covers the ability to think calmly and wisely.

Arjuna’s earlier question about why people act against their own wisdom is answered more clearly here. The problem is not lack of understanding. The problem is that desire clouds the mind and pushes a person toward actions that promise immediate satisfaction, even if they bring long-term harm.

This insight reveals a major challenge in human psychology. People may know the right path, yet repeated cravings—whether for recognition, pleasure, success, or control—pull them away from it. Recognizing desire as a powerful internal force is the first step toward regaining control over one’s decisions and restoring clarity of thought.
