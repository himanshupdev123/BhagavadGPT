# Chapter 3, Verse 38

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
धूमेनाव्रियते वह्निर्यथाऽऽदर्शो मलेन च।

यथोल्बेनावृतो गर्भस्तथा तेनेदमावृतम्।।3.38।।

**English Translation:**
As fire is covered by smoke, as a mirror is covered by dust, and as an embryo is covered in the womb, in the same way knowledge is covered by this desire.

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna explains how desire hides a person’s natural clarity. He uses three images to show different levels of obstruction. Fire covered by smoke still gives light but not clearly. A mirror covered by dust cannot reflect properly until it is cleaned. An embryo in the womb is completely hidden and not yet visible. These examples illustrate how knowledge can be partially or fully obscured by desire.

The mind naturally possesses the ability to understand right from wrong. However, when strong desires dominate the mind, this clarity becomes clouded. Sometimes the obstruction is mild, like smoke over fire, where a person still understands the truth but struggles to follow it. In other cases it is heavier, like dust on a mirror, where judgment becomes distorted. In the worst case, desire completely blocks awareness, like an embryo hidden in the womb, preventing a person from seeing the consequences of their actions at all.

This teaching directly relates to Arjuna’s earlier question about why people act against their own wisdom. Desire does not destroy knowledge completely, but it covers it. A person may know what is right, yet the pull of craving can temporarily hide that understanding.

The verse shows that the real task is not to create knowledge but to remove what obscures it. When the mind becomes clearer and less dominated by craving, wisdom naturally begins to guide action.

**Modern Applications:**
desire clouding judgment, loss of clarity, emotional bias in decision making, self-awareness, removing mental distractions, psychology of temptation
