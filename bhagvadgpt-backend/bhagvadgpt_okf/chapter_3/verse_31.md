# Chapter 3, Verse 31

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
ये मे मतमिदं नित्यमनुतिष्ठन्ति मानवाः।
श्रद्धावन्तोऽनसूयन्तो मुच्यन्ते तेऽपि कर्मभिः।।3.31।।

**English Translation:**
Those who constantly practice My teachings with full faith and without envy are freed from the bondage of their actions.

**Meaning & Purport:**
This verse highlights the psychological importance of trust and openness when adopting a new way of living. It suggests that for any profound philosophy to work, one must approach it with two specific mental qualities: conviction (faith) and a lack of cynicism (non-envy). When we constantly second-guess or look for reasons to criticize a constructive path, we create mental friction that prevents us from actually experiencing its benefits.

Just as Arjuna felt paralyzed by his attachments and duties, his initial hesitation was fueled by doubt and a defensive ego. Krishna points out that liberation from the 'bondage of action'—the feeling of being trapped by responsibilities and the anxiety of results—comes to those who stop resisting the truth and start applying it consistently. 

In modern life, we often suffer from 'analysis paralysis' or a habitual skepticism that keeps us stuck in old, destructive patterns. We might know what we need to do to improve our lives, but we let envy of others or a lack of trust in the process hold us back. This verse teaches that by following a proven principle with a sincere and non-judgmental heart, we naturally shed the stress and weight of our daily tasks. Freedom isn't found by escaping work, but by changing our psychological relationship with it through steady, faithful practice.

**Modern Applications:**
consistency, growth mindset, overcoming cynicism, trust, liberation, integrity
