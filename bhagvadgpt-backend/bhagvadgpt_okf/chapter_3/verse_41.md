# Chapter 3, Verse 41

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
तस्मात्त्वमिन्द्रियाण्यादौ नियम्य भरतर्षभ।

पाप्मानं प्रजहि ह्येनं ज्ञानविज्ञाननाशनम्।।3.41।।

**English Translation:**
Therefore, O best of the Bharatas, first control the senses and destroy this sinful force of desire, which destroys knowledge and wisdom.

**Meaning & Purport:**
After explaining how desire clouds knowledge and operates through the senses, mind, and intellect, Krishna now gives a practical instruction. The battle against destructive desire must begin at the level of the senses. The senses are the entry points through which attraction begins. When they are allowed to wander freely toward tempting objects, the mind quickly becomes attached, and the intellect eventually loses clarity.

Krishna tells Arjuna that discipline must begin early in this chain. If a person waits until desire fully dominates the mind, it becomes much harder to control. By regulating sensory exposure and habits, the mind remains steadier and less vulnerable to strong cravings. This form of control is not suppression but intelligent management of attention and behavior.

In Arjuna’s situation, emotional reactions and attachments were already disturbing his judgment about his duty. Krishna guides him to regain control by strengthening awareness and discipline rather than allowing his senses and emotions to dictate his decisions.

The verse highlights an important psychological strategy: preventing problems is easier than fixing them after they grow stronger. When a person consciously manages what they repeatedly see, hear, and focus on, the mind remains clearer. This clarity protects both knowledge and practical wisdom from being overwhelmed by impulsive desires.

**Modern Applications:**
self-discipline, managing temptations, habit control, attention management, preventing impulsive behavior, protecting mental clarity, self-regulation
