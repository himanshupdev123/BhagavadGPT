# Chapter 3, Verse 40

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
इन्द्रियाणि मनो बुद्धिरस्याधिष्ठानमुच्यते।

एतैर्विमोहयत्येष ज्ञानमावृत्य देहिनम्।।3.40।।

**English Translation:**
The senses, the mind, and the intellect are said to be the places where this desire resides. Through them, it covers knowledge and confuses the embodied person.

**Meaning & Purport:**
Krishna now explains where the force of desire operates within a person. It does not exist as something outside the individual; it works through the senses, the mind, and the intellect. The senses seek enjoyable experiences from the external world. The mind begins to dwell on these experiences and develop emotional attraction toward them. The intellect, which should guide judgment, becomes influenced and begins to justify those desires. Through this process, clarity becomes clouded.

This description reveals a chain reaction inside human psychology. First the senses encounter something attractive. Then the mind repeatedly thinks about it, strengthening the desire. Eventually the intellect, which normally distinguishes right from wrong, becomes biased and supports the craving. Once this happens, knowledge becomes ineffective, and the person acts under the influence of impulse rather than reason.

Arjuna’s earlier confusion about why people behave against their own wisdom is explained here in practical terms. The disturbance does not begin at the level of action; it begins earlier within perception and thought. If these early stages are not controlled, the intellect loses its ability to guide behavior.

The verse highlights the importance of awareness. By recognizing how desire moves through the senses, mind, and intellect, a person can interrupt the cycle before it fully takes control of their decisions.
