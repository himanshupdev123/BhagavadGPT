# Chapter 3, Verse 30

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
मयि सर्वाणि कर्माणि संन्यस्याध्यात्मचेतसा।
निराशीर्निर्ममो भूत्वा युध्यस्व विगतज्वरः।।3.30।।

**English Translation:**
Surrendering all your actions unto Me, with your mind focused on the highest self, free from desire and selfishness, fight without any mental fever.

**Meaning & Purport:**
This verse offers the ultimate psychological framework for peak performance under high-pressure conditions. It describes a state of 'mental coolness' (vigata-jvarah) achieved through three specific internal shifts: dedicating the work to a higher cause, letting go of the obsession with personal gain, and dropping the 'owner' complex.

Just as Arjuna felt paralyzed by his attachments and duties—shaking with a literal and metaphorical fever of anxiety about the war’s outcome—modern individuals often suffer from 'mental burnout' caused by over-identifying with their tasks. We carry the heavy weight of 'I am doing this' and 'What will I get from this?' which creates a friction that slows us down and drains our energy.

By adopting the mindset suggested here, you stop viewing your work as a burden for your ego and start viewing it as a contribution to a larger whole. This shift doesn't make you less effective; it makes you more lethal in your execution. When you are no longer distracted by the feverish 'what-ifs' of success or failure, your focus becomes laser-sharp. You can 'fight' your daily battles with total intensity because your mind is no longer leaking energy through anxiety or greed. True power comes from being fully engaged in the action while being internally rested in a higher purpose.

**Modern Applications:**
peak performance, burnout prevention, anxiety management, detachment, flow state, purpose-driven work
