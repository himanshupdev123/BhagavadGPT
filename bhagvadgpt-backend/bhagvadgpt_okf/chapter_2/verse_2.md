# Chapter 2, Verse 2

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
श्री भगवानुवाच

कुतस्त्वा कश्मलमिदं विषमे समुपस्थितम्।

अनार्यजुष्टमस्वर्ग्यमकीर्तिकरमर्जुन।।2.2।।

**English Translation:**
The Supreme Lord said: O Arjuna, how has this weakness come upon you at such a critical moment? It is not worthy of a noble person. It does not lead to higher good, and it brings only dishonor.

**Meaning & Purport:**
In this verse, Krishna speaks for the first time in response to Arjuna’s emotional collapse. Instead of immediately comforting him, Krishna directly challenges Arjuna’s state of mind. He questions how such weakness could arise in a moment that demands clarity and strength.

Krishna describes Arjuna’s mindset as unworthy of a noble person. The word “arya” refers to someone who lives with discipline, courage, and responsibility. Krishna is pointing out that Arjuna’s current attitude—giving up his duty due to emotional overwhelm—does not align with the values he has been trained to uphold. Krishna also states that this behavior will not lead to higher well-being and will only damage Arjuna’s honor and reputation.

The psychological insight here is direct: emotional compassion can become unhealthy when it leads to avoidance of responsibility. Arjuna believes his refusal to fight is morally superior, but Krishna sees it as confusion disguised as virtue.

Just as Arjuna feels paralyzed by emotional conflict, modern individuals often experience moments where fear, guilt, or attachment cause them to abandon responsibilities. Krishna’s response shows that true wisdom sometimes requires confronting emotional weakness rather than justifying it.

**Modern Applications:**
emotional weakness, avoiding responsibility, moral confusion, courage, duty, self-discipline
