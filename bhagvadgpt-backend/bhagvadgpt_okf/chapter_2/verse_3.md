# Chapter 2, Verse 3

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
क्लैब्यं मा स्म गमः पार्थ नैतत्त्वय्युपपद्यते।

क्षुद्रं हृदयदौर्बल्यं त्यक्त्वोत्तिष्ठ परन्तप।।2.3।।

**English Translation:**
O Partha, do not give in to this weakness. It does not suit you. Give up this petty faint-heartedness and arise, O conqueror of enemies.

**Meaning & Purport:**
In this verse, Krishna continues addressing Arjuna’s mental collapse with direct and forceful language. He tells Arjuna not to surrender to weakness and reminds him that such faint-heartedness is unworthy of him. Krishna refers to Arjuna as Partha and Parantapa—names that remind him of his strength and reputation as a powerful warrior who has defeated many enemies.

Krishna’s words reveal an important psychological principle. When a person becomes overwhelmed by emotional distress, the mind can begin to identify with weakness and helplessness. Arjuna, who was once confident and capable, now sees himself as unable to act. Krishna interrupts this mental pattern by reminding him of his true strength and character.

The phrase “heart’s weakness” refers to the emotional paralysis that prevents a person from fulfilling necessary responsibilities. Krishna is not dismissing Arjuna’s compassion but pointing out that compassion should not turn into avoidance of duty.

Just as Arjuna becomes trapped in self-doubt and emotional weakness, modern individuals often experience similar moments when fear or guilt causes them to withdraw from important responsibilities. Krishna’s instruction emphasizes that clarity and courage must sometimes replace emotional hesitation in order to act responsibly.

**Modern Applications:**
self-doubt, emotional weakness, courage, duty, overcoming fear, mental resilience
