# Chapter 2, Verse 1

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
सञ्जय उवाच

तं तथा कृपयाऽविष्टमश्रुपूर्णाकुलेक्षणम्।

विषीदन्तमिदं वाक्यमुवाच मधुसूदनः।।2.1।।

**English Translation:**
Sanjaya said: Seeing Arjuna overwhelmed with compassion, his eyes filled with tears and his mind deeply troubled with grief, Madhusudana (Krishna) spoke the following words.

**Meaning & Purport:**
This verse marks the beginning of the philosophical dialogue that forms the heart of the Bhagavad Gita. Sanjaya describes Arjuna’s emotional state just before Krishna begins to speak. Arjuna is overwhelmed with compassion, his eyes filled with tears, and his mind deeply disturbed by grief and confusion.

The description emphasizes that Arjuna is not simply sad; he is psychologically paralyzed. His sense of duty as a warrior has collapsed under the pressure of emotional attachment and moral uncertainty. The warrior who once stood confidently on the battlefield is now unable to act or think clearly. His compassion for his relatives has turned into overwhelming sorrow that clouds his judgment.

Just as Arjuna becomes immobilized by emotional conflict, modern individuals often experience similar moments when faced with difficult responsibilities. When emotions such as fear, guilt, or attachment become too strong, the mind loses clarity and the ability to make balanced decisions. A person may feel trapped between competing values and unable to move forward.

This verse therefore captures the precise moment when guidance becomes necessary. Arjuna’s breakdown creates the conditions for learning. Recognizing that Arjuna’s mind is overwhelmed, Krishna is about to begin addressing the deeper questions of duty, identity, and wisdom that Arjuna cannot resolve on his own.

**Modern Applications:**
emotional overwhelm, decision paralysis, grief, moral confusion, need for guidance, inner conflict
