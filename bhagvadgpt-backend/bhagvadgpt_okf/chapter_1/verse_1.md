# Chapter 1, Verse 1

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
धृतराष्ट्र उवाच
धर्मक्षेत्रे कुरुक्षेत्रे समवेता युयुत्सवः।
मामकाः पाण्डवाश्चैव किमकुर्वत सञ्जय।।1.1।।

**English Translation:**
Dhritarashtra said: O Sanjaya, after my sons and the sons of Pandu assembled in the place of pilgrimage at Kurukshetra, desiring to fight, what did they do?

**Meaning & Purport:**
This opening verse of the Bhagavad Gita sets the physical and psychological stage for the great war. The blind King Dhritarashtra asks his secretary, Sanjaya, about the events taking place on the battlefield of Kurukshetra. The king uses the word mamakah meaning my sons, in contrast to the sons of Pandu. This simple distinction reveals his deep-seated bias and attachment. He separates his own children from his nephews, forgetting that as the ruling elder, he was meant to protect both equally.

Because Kurukshetra is described as dharma-kshetra (a place of righteousness), Dhritarashtra is secretly anxious. He fears that the holy nature of the land might influence his corrupt sons to agree to a peaceful settlement, or that it will naturally favor the righteous Pandavas, leading to his own family's defeat.

This verse serves as a pure narrative anchor. It introduces us to the root cause of the impending conflict: a leader's blindness, not just physically, but morally and psychologically, driven by selfish attachment. It establishes the historical context and initiates the dialogue that will soon frame Arjuna's emotional breakdown and the deep philosophical teachings that follow.
