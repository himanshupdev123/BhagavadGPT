# Chapter 8, Verse 15

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
मामुपेत्य पुनर्जन्म दुःखालयमशाश्वतम्।
नाप्नुवन्ति महात्मानः संसिद्धिं परमां गताः।।8.15।।

**English Translation:**
After attaining Me, the great souls, who are yogis in devotion, never return to this temporary world, which is full of miseries, because they have attained the highest perfection.

**Meaning & Purport:**
This verse delivers the harshest, most necessary reality check in the entire Gita. Krishna completely obliterates the modern obsession with 'toxic positivity' by bluntly defining the exact nature of the material world.

Just as Arjuna felt paralyzed by his attachments and duties, desperately hoping to somehow engineer a perfect, bloodless, happy ending on the battlefield where everyone lived and he got his kingdom back in peace, modern individuals are constantly frustrated because they expect the world to be fair, comfortable, and permanently satisfying. We furiously try to optimize our lives to completely eliminate pain. Because we falsely believe that the default state of the world should be 'happy,' every single setback, disease, or heartbreak feels like a shocking, unfair betrayal by the universe.

Krishna clinically diagnoses the material world as 'duhkhalayam' (a place of misery) and 'ashashvatam' (temporary). This is not pessimism; it is absolute, liberating realism. The material world is literally designed to break down, cause friction, and eventually end. If you expect a permanent utopia here, you will be constantly crushed. The 'mahatmanah' (great souls) accept this brutal fact without complaining. By fully acknowledging that the material simulation is inherently flawed and temporary, they stop desperately trying to build a permanent home in a burning building. They redirect 100% of their energy toward attaining the ultimate, permanent perfection outside the cycle.

**Modern Applications:**
toxic positivity, radical acceptance, the baseline of reality, escaping the matrix, managing expectations
