# Chapter 7, Verse 1

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**
श्री भगवानुवाच
मय्यासक्तमनाः पार्थ योगं युञ्जन्मदाश्रयः।
असंशयं समग्रं मां यथा ज्ञास्यसि तच्छृणु।।7.1।।

**English Translation:**
The Supreme Lord said: Now hear, O son of Pritha, how by practicing yoga with your mind fully attached to Me, and taking exclusive refuge in Me, you can know Me completely, free from all doubt.

**Meaning & Purport:**
This verse marks a massive psychological pivot in the Gita. Krishna shifts from teaching the behavioral mechanics of action and self-discipline to revealing the ultimate power of radical, focused attachment.

Just as Arjuna felt paralyzed by his attachments and duties, understanding the logic of his responsibilities but still plagued by deep, lingering uncertainties, modern individuals suffer heavily from 'analysis paralysis.' We consume endless podcasts, read countless self-help books, and test numerous productivity hacks, yet we remain fundamentally unsure of our direction. We try to intellectualize our way out of confusion, but because our minds are unanchored and heavily distracted by a thousand different worldly goals, we are constantly crippled by self-doubt. We know the 'how' of our daily grind, but we lack the absolute certainty of the 'why.'

Krishna introduces the ultimate cognitive anchor: 'mayy asakta-manah' (a mind intensely attached to the Supreme). He explains that you cannot permanently defeat doubt merely by thinking harder or optimizing your routine. You destroy doubt by radically shifting your ultimate point of refuge. When you stop relying on your own fragile ego or unpredictable external circumstances for security, and instead attach your deep focus entirely to the highest, unchanging reality, your crippling uncertainties simply vanish. You transition from a fractured, anxious mindset to 'samagram'—a complete, holistic, and completely doubtless understanding of your life and purpose.

**Modern Applications:**
analysis paralysis, cognitive anchoring, overcoming self-doubt, absolute certainty, focus and attachment
